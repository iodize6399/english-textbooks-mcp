RESPONSE

This is a curious but not uncommon trick of conversation combining the principles of direct and reported speech in one. Sometimes a remark is passed, the exact meaning of which is not fully grasped at the moment; after a short lapse of time the listener reverts to the speaker's original subject, asking about the doubtful point, but usually framing his DIRECT question in the tense of a REPORTED QUESTION with a past tense introducing verb. Examples will make this clearer – the words in italics are those the listener is questioning.

Mr A. I'm supposed to go again on Friday.

[Pause, or more miscellaneous conversation.]

Mr B. When were you supposed to go again?

(= When did you say you were supposed to go again.)

Mr A. His name is Tanner-Whyte.

[Pause; or conversation.]
