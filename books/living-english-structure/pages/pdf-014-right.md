Words in apposition: here the last word in the group usually takes the 's.

Hicks the plumber's daughter

(Compare: Betty, the plumber's daughter)

Of may replace almost any possessive case. It is useful for avoiding a complicated series of —'s.

'I am my friend's sister's second child's godmother'

although it is a fantastic example hardly likely to occur, is better expressed

I'm godmother to the second child of my friend's sister.

Of is the normal possessive for inanimate objects. Exceptions are certain accepted idioms, mostly of time or measure: I live a stone's throw from here; out of harm's way; three days' holiday; I'm at my wits' end.
