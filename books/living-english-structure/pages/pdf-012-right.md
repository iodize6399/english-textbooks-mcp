6.3

Intermediate CASE

1 When a pronoun is not felt to be the active subject of a sentence, it is normally found in the objective form. This is sometimes called the DISJUNCTIVE or SEPARATED pronoun. (Compare the French C'est moi, etc.)

Who's there? It's only me!

That's him over there!

If I were her, I wouldn't listen to him.

(Showing a photo) ... and this is me (standing) in front of the Louvre.

2 Between and let require the objective case after them.

Let him have something to eat.

There was an argument between him and me.
