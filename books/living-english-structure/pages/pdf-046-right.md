23.4
Elementary
PRESENT
PERFECT
CONTINUOUS

Sometimes an action, beginning indefinitely in the past, is still continuing at the present moment. This frequently happens with verbs of a static nature, such as stay, wait, sit, stand, lie, study, learn, live, rest, etc. Such verbs are rarely found in the simple PRESENT PERFECT, because by their very nature they continue into the present. So has lain, etc. is rare; has been lying, etc., common.

If we do not suggest duration from the past, the PRESENT CONTINUOUS is used; but as soon as we imply a relationship between the past and NOW, the PERFECT CONTINUOUS must be used.

He is lying on the floor. (NOW)

He has been lying there for three hours. (= and there he is still)

I'm writing a letter to my friend. (NOW)

I've written three letters since breakfast. (= completed up to NOW)

I've been writing letters all the morning. (= and I'm still writing NOW)
