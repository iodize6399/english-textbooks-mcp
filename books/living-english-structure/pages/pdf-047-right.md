Notice:

He has gone to London.

He has been to London.

Students are often puzzled by this apparent misuse of the preposition to after the verb be. Actually I have been must be considered as part of the verb go, an extension of I have gone with the meaning 'I have gone and come back again.' So

He has gone to London

– and that's where you can find him now

He has been to London

– and so he can tell you what he did there; he knows London now.
