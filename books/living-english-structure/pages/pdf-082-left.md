# **31.10  
Advanced  
THE UNREAL  
PAST**

It will be noticed that in TYPES (2) and (3) of the conditional (see Exercise 31.1), the PAST SIMPLE or PAST PERFECT tense is used when we suppose what is impossible. These are really subjunctives, but seeing that *were* is the only visible sign of this form that occurs with any frequency, the student needn't be asked to differentiate between past subjunctive and past indicative. The PAST SIMPLE tense is used for something unreal or wished-for NOW, and the PAST PERFECT when the supposition or wish was all in the past. Other expressions using the past tenses in this way are: *I wish, as if, if only, would to God, suppose, it's (high) time, I'd rather.*

If only I *knew* the answer (now)! If only I *had known* (yesterday)!
