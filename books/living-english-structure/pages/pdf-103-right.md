This difference is largely imaginary, but is argued presumably as follows:

They say that in (1) the liking is a present one, and in (2) it is past. This idea is clearer if we consider carefully the following pair:

(1) I should like to have gone. (What a pity I didn't!)
(2) I should have liked to go (if only I had known about it at the time).

The PERFECT INFINITIVE is commonly used to express an unfulfilled hope or expectation:

I hoped to have seen you before now.

We meant to have stayed there a week.

Henry was to have bought some wine ... (but he forgot).
