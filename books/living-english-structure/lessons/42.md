## 42 Verb, Noun or Adjective

<!-- living-english-structure:pdf-104-left:03 | printed p. 196 | PDF p. 104 left -->

### 42.1 Elementary

Many common words can be used as both verbs and nouns. Because they occur more frequently as verbs, students are apt to get less practice in their use as nouns.

* * *

- Make two sentences with each of the following words: (a) as a verb; (b) as a noun:
cook; bite; swim; walk; answer; drink; rain; rest; work; smell; shave; smile; use¹; burn; cut; fight; pay; play; brush; dress; sleep; watch; kiss; look.

<!-- living-english-structure:pdf-104-left:04 | printed p. 196 | PDF p. 104 left -->

### 42.2 Intermediate

- Make sentences with each of the following words: (a) as a verb; (b) as a noun:
burn; cough; fall; fear; copy; fold; light; drive; mark; smoke; sneeze; show; stand; taste; break; call; climb; cover; fly; help; kick; measure; move; colour; water; arm; hand; damage; place; act; dust; iron.

¹ v. [ju:z], n. [ju:s]

**42.3**

change; doubt; jump; laugh; lift; start; stop; turn; step; rub; shake; tear; touch; want; bend; catch; draw; hold; leave; make; air; book; shoe; stomach; mouth¹; finger; thumb; eye; steam; house²; match; dog; harbour.

**42.4**
**Elementary** ● Make two sentences with each of the following words: (a) as an adjective; (b) as a verb:
dry; open; shut; warm; clean; hurt; wet.

**42.5**
