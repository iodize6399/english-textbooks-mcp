## 50 there is and it is

<!-- living-english-structure:pdf-126-right:03 | printed p. 241 | PDF p. 126 right -->

**50.1** *There is, there are.* This is the most usual way of
Elementary denoting existence in English, when the subject has not
PREPARATORY already been defined.

*THERE*

The two books are on the table. (We know which books.)

There are two books on the table. (Location of two unknown books.)

'Two books are on the table' is a grammatically possible but very rare form.

50.2 It is, was, etc. These expressions are useful for introducing a complex subject (phrase or clause), which would sound very clumsy if made the real subject.

IT

It's impossible to guess her age.

It is wrong not to do as you are told.

# **Intermediate**

See notes to Exercises 50.1–3.

Notice the following expressions:

- (a) It is time to go. (= The correct moment has arrived.)
- (b) It is a long time since . . . (= The time has been long.)
- (c) There is time to go. (= We have time; enough time exists.)
- (d) It's a long way to Tipperary. (= The distance between here and Tipperary is long.)
- (e) There's a long way to go yet before we get there. (= We still have this distance more to go; such a distance still exists for us.)
