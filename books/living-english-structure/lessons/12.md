# 12.1

# Elementary

# Compare:

Come nowhere near me!

Don't come anywhere near me!

Both are grammatically correct, but the not . . . any form is the normal one for ordinary statements. In fact several of the sentences below sound unusual in speech.

## 12.2 Elementary

The *no*-forms are chiefly used as short negative answers to questions.

Where are you going? – Nowhere.

Remember: *neither* of two; *none* of many.

\* \* \*

- ● Answer the following questions in the negative; respond quickly:
  1. 1 Where are you going?
  2. 2 How many exercises have you done today?
  3. 3 Who were you talking to?
  4. 4 How much did these flowers cost?

## 12.3 Intermediate

Read notes on Exercise 12.1.

Notice that *neither* = 'not either'.

Students should be reminded that several of the sentences below sound unusual in speech.

\* \* \*

- ● Reword the following *no*-sentences in the *not* . . . *any* form:

  3. 3 He gave his wife no money for her clothes.
  4. 4 They went nowhere after supper.
